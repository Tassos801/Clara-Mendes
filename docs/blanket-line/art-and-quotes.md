Rule applied (plan section 0.3): each quote has the artist's original-language text, its source, and an English wording that is either by a translator who died before 1956 or a new Clara Mendes translation made here from the original. No modern published translation was used. All English wordings below are new Clara Mendes translations.

## Summary

1. **10 rows cleared, 1 needs an owner decision, 3 have no authenticated quote.** "Cleared" means that the source and translation are clean. The owner still approves each line for the design.
2. **All three proof quotes are cleared.** Hokusai (1834 postscript, checked against a facsimile), Mondrian (1914 letter to Bremmer), Kandinsky (1912 German text, p. 49, checked against a scan of the 3rd edition).
3. **The Kandinsky working line "Colour is a means of touching the soul directly" is not faithful.** The German says *einen direkten Einfluß auf die Seele auszuüben*, which means "exert a direct influence on the soul". Use the wording in row 5.

5. **The scope is still 14 rows.** `data/art-registry.json` on this branch has the 11 museum artworks, and `app/lib/clothingArtists.ts` has the 3 inspired lines. No additions or removals.

## The three proof designs in full

**Hokusai: *Under the Wave off Kanagawa (The Great Wave)*, c. 1830–32 (`met-45434`)**
- Original: 百有十歳にしては一点一格にして生るがごとくならん
- Source: Hokusai, postscript (跋) to *Fugaku hyakkei* (One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji), vol. 1, 1834. The postscript is signed 画狂老人卍述, "related by the Old Man Mad about Painting, Manji". The transcription is from Nagata Seiji, *Katsushika Hokusai* (Yoshikawa Kōbunkan, 2000), pp. 188–189, as cited in [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/富嶽百景_(北斎)). I compared the sentence with the facsimile of the colophon page on [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hugaku100kei_batsubun.jpg) and it matches. Full digitised set: [University of Tsukuba](https://dc.tulips.tsukuba.ac.jp/s/pub_ja/document/rb_10076868949).
- English (Clara Mendes): **"At a hundred and ten, every dot and every stroke will seem alive."** (65 characters)
- Credit: Katsushika Hokusai, 1834 (our translation)
- Note: ja.wikipedia transcribes an earlier clause as 八十六才, but the facsimile appears to read 八十才. That clause is not part of the quote.

**Mondrian: *Composition with Red, Yellow, and Blue*, 1927 (`cma-1967.215`)**
- Original (Dutch, 1914 spelling): ik construeer op een plat vlak lijnen en kleurcombinaties met 't doel de algemeene schoonheid zoo bewust mogelijk uit te beelden.
- Source: Mondrian, letter to H. P. Bremmer, Paris, 26 rue du Départ, 29 January 1914. First published in J. Joosten, "Documentatie over Mondriaan (1)", *Museumjournaal* 12/4 (1968), pp. 210–212. I read it as quoted in L. Veen, "Piet Mondriaans eerste teksten (1914)", *Streven* 83 (2016), p. 416: [DBNL](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_str010201601_01/_str010201601_01_0044.php).
- English (Clara Mendes): **"On a flat plane I construct lines and colour combinations, to show universal beauty as consciously as I can."** (107 characters, two lines)
- Short form if the strip needs it: **"On a flat plane I construct lines and colour combinations."** (58 characters; leaves out the purpose clause)
- Words left out: the opening "Want" ("For").
- Credit: Piet Mondrian, letter, 1914 (our translation)
- *Algemeene schoonheid* literally means "general beauty". "Universal" follows Mondrian's later term *universele schoonheid*. Avoid the familiar English wording "...general beauty with the utmost awareness": its translator is unknown, so treat it as protected.

**Kandinsky: *Houses at Murnau*, 1909 (`aic-129849`)**
- Original (German, 1912): Im allgemeinen ist also die Farbe ein Mittel, einen direkten Einfluß auf die Seele auszuüben.
- Source: Kandinsky, *Über das Geistige in der Kunst*, 3rd edition, Munich: R. Piper & Co., 1912, chapter V "Wirkung der Farbe", p. 49. Page checked on the [scan at archive.org](https://archive.org/details/kandinsky_202603) (leaf 65) and against the [Project Gutenberg text #46203](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46203), which reproduces the same edition.
- English (Clara Mendes): **"Colour is a means of exerting a direct influence on the soul."** (61 characters)
- Words left out: "Im allgemeinen ist also" ("Generally speaking, then").
- Alternative from the next three sentences, nothing left out: "Die Farbe ist die Taste. Das Auge ist der Hammer. Die Seele ist das Klavier mit vielen Saiten." In English: **"Colour is the key. The eye is the hammer. The soul is the piano with many strings."** (83 characters)
- Credit: Wassily Kandinsky, 1912 (our translation)
- Do not print the reference image's wording. "Colour is a power which directly influences the soul" is Sadler's translation (Sadler died in 1957), which is still protected in the EU. The working line "touching the soul directly" is a paraphrase, not a translation.

- **Credits.** Rows 1–11: the quote credit names the artist and year, followed by "(our translation)" where translated. Rows 12–14: the design credit stays "Inspired by …", and the quote credit names the historical artist as the author of the words only. - **Length.** Mondrian (107 characters) and Van Gogh (96) are over the 90-character aim. Both work as two lines; Mondrian also has a 58-character short form.
